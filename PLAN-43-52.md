## Research

- **Times-table patterns (3.OA.9).** Even × any number is even; odd × odd is odd; turnarounds mirror across the
  table's diagonal; doubling one factor doubles the product (4 × 6 is double 2 × 6); ×9 is ×10 take away one group,
  so the tens go up and the ones go down; ×5 is half of ×10.
- **Mass and liquid volume (3.MD.2).** Grams for light things (a paper clip is about 1 g), kilograms for heavy things
  (1 kg = 1000 g); liters for liquid. Measure by balancing gram weights and by reading a marked beaker, then solve
  one-step problems with all four operations. A beaker is a measuring tool like the rulers the founder allowed, not a
  number line. Sources: [CDE 3.MD.2](https://www2.cde.ca.gov/cacs/id/web/352),
  [NYS grade 3 module 2](https://www.scribd.com/document/195047453/Math-g3-m2-Full-Module),
  [Varsity Tutors: mass and volume](https://www.varsitytutors.com/practice/subjects/3rd-grade-math/help/measure-and-calculate-mass-and-volume).
- **Scaled graphs (3.MD.3).** Picture graphs where each picture stands for 2, 5 or 10, and bar graphs whose scale counts
  by 2s or 5s; answer "how many more" and "how many less" questions.
- **Quarter inches (3.MD.4).** Rulers marked in halves and fourths; measure to the nearest quarter inch; show the
  measurements in an X chart (line plot) with fraction labels.
- **Quadrilaterals (3.G.1).** Shapes share attributes (4 sides, 4 corners); squares, rectangles and rhombuses are
  quadrilaterals; a square is a rectangle and a rhombus; draw quadrilaterals that are none of these.
- **Thousands (4.NBT.1–2).** A digit is worth ten times the same digit one place to its right; read, write and compare
  numbers in base-ten pictures, expanded form and digits.
- **Rounding big numbers (4.NBT.3).** The same founder's method: the thousand below, the thousand above, which is
  closer (the ten-frame now counts hundreds).
- **Adding and subtracting big numbers (4.NBT.4).** The column method of packets 13, 14 and 29 with more places.
- **Multiplying by one digit (4.NBT.5).** Area model first (23 × 4 = 20 × 4 + 3 × 4), then partial products in a list,
  then plain problems; three-digit numbers after two-digit. Sources: [CDE 4.NBT.5](https://www2.cde.ca.gov/cacs/id/web/374),
  [Fishtank grade 4 multi-digit multiplication](https://fishtanklearning.org/curriculum/math/4th-grade/multi-digit-multiplication/),
  [HCPSS 4.NBT.5](https://hcpss.instructure.com/courses/107/modules/items/17532).
- **Division with remainders (4.OA.3).** Share and see what is left over; use a times fact to find the quotient and the
  remainder; decide what the remainder means in a story (leave it, need one more, it is the answer). Build it from
  context, not rules. Sources: [Core Standards 4.OA.3](https://thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/4/OA/A/3/),
  [NYSED 4.OA.3 unpacking](https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/curriculum-instruction/unpacking-template-ny-4-oa-3.pdf),
  [HCPSS 4.OA.3](https://hcpss.instructure.com/courses/107/modules/items/17519).
