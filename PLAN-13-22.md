# WeMath packets 13–22: research and plan

## Research summary

- **Common Core grade 2** extends place value to hundreds (2.NBT.1). It asks kids to:
  - skip-count by 5s, 10s and 100s (2.NBT.2)
  - compare three-digit numbers with <, > and = (2.NBT.4)
  - find 10 or 100 more or less (2.NBT.8)
  - add and subtract within 1000 by place value: hundreds with hundreds, tens with tens, ones with ones, composing or decomposing a ten or hundred when needed (2.NBT.7)
- **Quick drawings** (Achieve the Core coherence map) show **a hundred as one square** (a "flat"), not as ten tens. A ten is a line and a one is a dot. Breaking a hundred into 10 tens is the three-digit version of packet 12's "break a ten".
- **Mathnasium's grade-2 notes** list counting by 10s and 100s, hundreds place value, and "up to three-digit subtraction with and without borrowing in a column format".
- **Usual grade-2 order for multiplication readiness** (Eureka Module 6, Fishtank Unit 7, Illustrative Math Unit 8): equal groups, then arrays and repeated addition (2.OA.4, up to 5 × 5), then even and odd by pairing objects and counting by 2s (2.OA.3). Even numbers are written as a double (7 + 7 = 14).

## Rules every packet follows (from CLAUDE.md)

- Page 1 is a visual learning page with a Your turn, plus a teacher note on the key.
- Help is removed step by step, ending in about 40 no-help problems grouped by kind, then 5 word problems with a challenge last.
- Equations use the a + b = c order with the blank in the answer spot. The exceptions are the packet's own core skill and place-value forms like 300 + ☐ + 5 = 345.
- No number lines.
- No question twice across packets. Each packet owns its core facts.
- Use a gray "N)" for problem numbers, keep about 15pt of space between layers inside a card, and check both inks and the answer key.
- Yes/no and choice cards keep the choices in a fixed order; only the questions are shuffled.

### 13. Adding in Columns

- **Goal:** write two-digit addition stacked, and carry the new ten.
- **Learning page:**
  - 34 + 25 on a mat next to the stacked problem.
  - 38 + 25: 8 + 5 = 13, the 3 stays and the ten moves to the tens column (a small 1).
  - Your turn: 47 + 36.
- **Practice:**
  1. Stack and add, no new ten (labeled columns).
  2. Line them up yourself (46 + 7 written into an empty T/O grid).
  3. New ten with the mat.
  4. New ten with a box for the carried 1.
- **No help, stacked:** no new ten, new ten, two-digit + one-digit, mixed.
- **Words:** 5.

### 14. Subtracting in Columns

- **Goal:** the written form of packet 12.
- **Learning page:**
  - 58 − 23 stacked, nothing to break.
  - 52 − 27 on the mat: a stick opens into 10 ones; cross out 5, write 4; cross out 2, write 12.
  - Your turn.
- **Practice:**
  1. No breaking (labeled columns).
  2. Break shown.
  3. Break it yourself (boxes above the digits).
- **No help, stacked:** no breaking, break a ten, from a tens number (70 − 26), mixed.
- **Words:** 5.

### 15. Hundreds, Tens and Ones

- **Goal:** three-digit place value.
- **Learning page:**
  - 10 tens make 1 hundred (10 sticks become a flat).
  - 243 as 2 flats, 4 sticks and 3 dots, with an H/T/O chart and 200 + 40 + 3.
  - Quick drawings.
  - Your turn.
- **Practice:**
  1. Count hundreds, tens and ones from pictures.
  2. Draw it with quick drawings.
  3. Hundreds + tens + ones (300 + 40 + 5 = ☐).
  4. Zeros matter (3 hundreds 0 tens 5 ones = ☐).
  5. What is the digit worth? (in 472, the 7 is worth ☐)
- **No help:** to a number, into hundreds/tens/ones, expanded form.
- **Words:** boxes of 100, packs of 10.

### 16. Skip Counting by 5s, 10s and 100s

- **Learning page:**
  - Bars of 5 (5, 10, 15 …).
  - Sticks (10, 20 …) and counting by 10s from any number (23, 33, 43).
  - Flats (100, 200 …) and counting from any hundred (140, 240).
  - The ones go 5, 0, 5, 0.
- **Practice:**
  1. Count the bars by 5s.
  2. Count on by 10s from a number.
  3. Count on by 100s.
  4. Keep going with jump bubbles.
- **No help:** fill in the missing numbers when counting by 5s, by 10s and by 100s; "What are we counting by?"
- **Words:** 5.

### 17. Comparing Numbers

- **Goal:** use <, > and = with two- and three-digit numbers.
- **Learning page:**
  - 46 vs 64 with sticks.
  - 352 vs 325 with quick drawings (same hundreds, so look at the tens).
  - The wide side faces the bigger number.
  - Your turn.
- **Practice:**
  1. Compare with pictures (two-digit).
  2. Compare with quick drawings (three-digit).
  3. Which place decides? Circle hundreds, tens or ones.
  4. Put three numbers in order.
- **No help:** two-digit, three-digit, same hundreds, zeros and equal (409 vs 490).
- **Words:** "Who has more?"

### 18. 10 and 100 More or Less

- **Learning page:**
  - 243 + 100 with an orange flat, so only the hundreds digit changes.
  - 243 − 100 with a flat crossed out.
  - 243 + 10 (the tens digit changes).
  - A number cross.
  - Your turn.
- **Practice:**
  1. 100 more with pictures.
  2. 100 less with pictures.
  3. Number crosses.
  4. ±100 and ±10 chains.
- **No help:** 100 more, 100 less, 10 more or less with three-digit numbers, mixed. A few cross a hundred (395 + 10); the teacher note mentions them.
- **Words:** 5.

### 19. Adding Three-Digit Numbers

- **Goal:** hundreds + hundreds, tens + tens, ones + ones, then total. A total row with three parts handles new tens and hundreds without carrying (300 + 70 + 12 = 382).
- **Learning page:**
  - 243 + 125.
  - 247 + 135 (the ones make a new ten).
  - Your turn: 362 + 254 (the tens make a new hundred).
- **Practice:**
  1. Add hundreds (300 + 200, 340 + 200).
  2. Rows with quick drawings.
  3. Rows with every box blank.
  4. Stack it (columns with carry boxes).
- **No help, stacked:** no new ten, new ten, new hundred, mixed.
- **Words:** 5.

### 20. Subtracting Three-Digit Numbers

- **Goal:** take away hundreds, tens and ones, breaking a ten or a hundred when there isn't enough. Only one break per problem; both breaks and zeros in the middle (403 − 157) are left for later.
- **Learning page:**
  - 468 − 135 (nothing to break).
  - 452 − 127 (break a ten: 400 + 40 + 12).
  - 528 − 263 (break a hundred: 400 + 120 + 8).
  - Your turn.
- **Practice:**
  1. Take away hundreds.
  2. Rows, nothing to break.
  3. Break a ten.
  4. Break a hundred.
  5. Stack it.
- **No help, stacked:** nothing to break, break a ten, break a hundred, mixed.
- **Words:** 5.

### 21. Equal Groups and Arrays

- **Learning page:**
  - 3 groups of 4 (circles of dots) = 4 + 4 + 4 = 12.
  - 3 rows of 4 (an array).
  - Read it two ways: 4 + 4 + 4 = 3 + 3 + 3 + 3.
  - Your turn.
- **Practice:**
  1. Equal groups from pictures.
  2. Equal or not? (yes/no)
  3. Arrays: rows of, plus the sum.
  4. Draw the array.
  5. Two ways.
- **No help:** add equal groups, rows of, skip-count the rows.
- **Words:** egg cartons, rows of chairs. Arrays go up to 5 × 5, and there's no × sign yet.

### 22. Even and Odd

- **Learning page:**
  - Pair them up: 8 makes pairs with none left (even); 7 has one left over (odd).
  - Even numbers are doubles (8 = 4 + 4).
  - Count by 2s.
  - The ones digit tells you (0, 2, 4, 6, 8).
  - Your turn.
- **Practice:**
  1. Pair the dots, then circle even or odd.
  2. Write it as a double.
  3. Count by 2s.
  4. Even or odd? by the ones digit.
  5. Odd is a double + 1.
- **No help:** even or odd, double it, count by 2s.
- **Words:** pairs and partners.

## Left for later (other strands)

Money, telling time, length, two-step word problems with bar models, both breaks and zeros in three-digit subtraction (403 − 157), and halves, thirds and fourths.
