# WeMath packets 26–30: research and plan

## The order, and why it changed

| # | Packet | Builds on | Standard |
|---|---|---|---|
| 26 | Halves, Thirds and Fourths | 21 (arrays), 25 (halves of numbers) | 2.G.3, 2.G.2 |
| 27 | Telling Time | 16 (count by 5s), 26 (halves and fourths of a circle) | 2.MD.7 |
| 28 | Money | 16 and 24 (count by 5s, 10s, 25s), 23 (hundred buddies: change from $1) | 2.MD.8 |
| 29 | Subtracting Across Zeros | 12, 14, 15, 20 (breaking a ten or a hundred) | 2.NBT.7 |
| 30 | Two-Step Word Problems | everything; reuses money and three-digit numbers | 2.OA.1 |

The old plan was Shapes → Money → Time → Two-Step → Across Zeros. Two changes:

- **Shapes come right before time.** Eureka grade 2 (Module 8) and Illustrative Math grade 2 (Unit 6) teach "half past" and "quarter past" as an application of halves and fourths of a circle. The time packet shades the clock face exactly like packet 26 shades shapes.
- **Across zeros comes before two-step problems.** It finishes three-digit subtraction (2.NBT.7), so the word-problem packet can lean on everything before it.

## What the research says, and how each packet uses it

- **Worked example → partly worked → on your own.** The IES practice guide *Organizing Instruction and Study* (Pashler et al. 2007, recommendation 2) says to alternate worked examples with problems kids solve alone, and to fade the examples. This is the founder's "training wheels" approach. Every packet keeps it.
- **Spaced and mixed review.** Same guide, recommendation 1: spaced review. In a randomized trial across 54 classes, mixed practice scored 61% against 38% for same-kind practice on a test a month later (Rohrer et al. 2019). **What changed:** each new packet ends with a short **"Remember?"** part of 6 fresh problems from earlier packets. Practice is still grouped by kind, as the founder wants.
- **Visual representations and schema-based word problems.** The IES 2021 guide *Assisting Students Struggling with Mathematics* recommends systematic instruction, clear math language, visual representations and word-problem schemas. The concrete → pictorial → abstract sequence has small but positive evidence (a meta-analytic review; see sources).
- **Keep one model across packets.** The bar model in packet 30 uses the same blue (first part) and orange (second part) as every dot picture since packet 1.

### 26. Halves, Thirds and Fourths

- **Standard 2.G.3:** split circles and rectangles into 2, 3 or 4 equal shares; name them (halves, thirds, fourths, half of, a third of); the whole is two halves, three thirds, four fourths; equal shares of the same whole don't need the same shape. No fraction numbers (½) in grade 2. **2.G.2:** rows and columns of same-size squares.
- **Misconceptions it targets:** counting pieces without checking they're equal (Part 1, cuts that look almost fair); thinking fourths are bigger because 4 > 2 (the "more shares, smaller shares" bars, Part 5, Part 10); thinking halves must look the same (learning page: three different halves of a square).
- **Pages:** learning page → equal shares? → name the share → shade it → cut it → which share is bigger → rows and columns → draw the squares → no help (make a whole, wholes and shares, <, > or =) → Remember? → 5 word problems.

### 27. Telling Time

- **Standard 2.MD.7:** tell and write time to the nearest 5 minutes, with a.m. and p.m. Elapsed time is grade 3 (3.MD.1), so it appears only in the challenge.
- **Mathnasium:** build on counting by 5s, 15s and 30s.
- **Misconceptions it targets:** reading the minute hand as the number it points at (the 5s ring around the clock fades from all numbers, to 15/30/45, to none); mixing up the hands (the hour hand is short, thick and blue; the minute hand is long, thin and orange); reading 2:50 as 3:50 (a whole part on "the short hand is between").
- **No number line:** counting by 5s goes around the clock face instead.

### 28. Money

- **Standard 2.MD.8:** word problems with dollar bills, quarters, dimes, nickels and pennies, using $ and ¢. Grade 2 uses cents or whole dollars. Decimal money ($3.45) is grade 4 (4.MD.2).
- **Mathnasium:** size and value are different (a dime is the smallest coin). Count by the coin's value, from the biggest coin, counting on from numbers that aren't multiples of 5.
- **Misconceptions it targets:** counting coins instead of their values; bigger coin means more money (coins are drawn at real relative size); mixing $ and ¢ (a whole "¢ or $?" part); not starting with the biggest coin (running-total boxes under each coin, then no help).

### 29. Subtracting Across Zeros

- **Standard 2.NBT.7:** subtract within 1000 by place value, decomposing tens or hundreds when needed.
- **Key idea:** 403 = 40 tens + 3 ones = 39 tens + 13 ones, so you break once instead of twice. After that, the tens never need another break (39 − 15). Packet 15 already taught "tens and ones" thinking.
- **Misconceptions it targets:** 0 − 7 = 7 (subtracting the smaller digit from the bigger); trying to borrow from the 0; breaking a hundred straight into ones. "Check by adding" closes the loop.
- **Pages:** tens and ones (two ways) → break, then subtract (rows, break given) → every box → stack it → no help: zero in the tens, zero in the ones, two zeros, mixed (some need no break at all) → check by adding.

### 30. Two-Step Word Problems

- **Standard 2.OA.1:** one- and two-step word problems within 100 (add to, take from, put together, take apart, compare), with the unknown in any spot, using drawings and equations. Eureka uses tape diagrams (bar models) throughout grade 2.
- **Bar rules:** part + part = whole (blue piece, orange piece, bracket above for the whole); compare (two bars, the gap dashed); two steps (the first answer becomes a part of the second bar).
- **Misconceptions it targets:** key-word traps ("Room 2 has 41 kids. That is 16 *fewer* than Room 3" means add); stopping after step 1; an unknown start.
- **Pages:** read the bar → part and part (bar given) → compare (bar given) → draw the bar → two steps (bars given) → two steps on your own (challenge last).

## Still to decide: measuring and graphs

The next standards (2.MD.1–6, 2.MD.9–10) are about measuring length and reading graphs. **A ruler is a number line**, and number lines were removed earlier. Options:

1. **Allow the ruler as a measuring tool only:** start with paper clips and cube trains (no gaps), then a ruler that starts at 0. No number-line adding (skip 2.MD.6).
2. **Units only, no ruler:** measure with drawn inch and centimeter cubes.

Graphs can avoid a number line entirely: picture graphs, bar graphs on a square grid, and "X charts" (labeled boxes with X's stacked above) instead of line plots.

**Grade 3 after that:** Multiplication (groups of → arrays → fact families by strategy: ×2 doubles, ×10, ×5 half of ×10, ×4 double-double, ×9 = ×10 minus a group) → Division as Sharing → Fractions as Numbers (with fraction strips instead of number lines).
